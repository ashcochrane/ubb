Tasks in the console

A wireframe for the two surfaces a kind of work implies — configuring it, and reporting on it. Rough on purpose: this exists to be argued with, not built from. Every element carries a marker pointing at the decision that forces it to be there.

2026-08-04 · revision 2 Renders the model decided in #138–#155 + #165 Contracts: #153 · Rendering: #152 Q1–Q4, Q6 accepted · Q5 ruled: declared kinds, explicit instances, observed composition

What is being drawn, and why it looks unfinished

Tasks have no surface in the console today. GET /api/v1/metering/analytics/tasks exists with no UI on top of it; PUT /api/v1/metering/task-types is the only way to declare a kind of work. Meanwhile sixteen decision documents have re-modelled what a task is — six lifecycle states, three ceilings, four spend-control families, four pricing statuses and a cost-completeness rule — and none of it has a consumer.

This wireframe takes a position on all six questions the ticket asks. The positions are proposals, not decisions; each one is marked with what I need a ruling on. The drawing is deliberately grey and mono — a polished mock would invite reactions about spacing when what is wanted is reactions about shape.

The rendering contract this surface exists to honour

Seven semantic states now have to be visibly different from each other and from zero. This is the whole reason the surface is hard, and it is what the fixtures must prove (#155 §9.2, extended by #165).

Why each element is here

  1. 1
    The aggregate lands, not the run list. A kind of work is the unit a tenant makes decisions about — price, ceiling, whether it is worth selling. A run list answers no question until you already have one.Q2 · position taken
  2. 2
    Synthetic charge postings excluded from every count: a task must not count its own invoice as a unit of work. The measure is recorded operations, never “events”.#153 §7.2
  3. 3
    The tenant-wide COGS total is a lower bound whenever any contributing cost is unresolved. “≥” plus the unresolved count, never a bare number.#151 §10 · #153 §10.5
  4. 4
    No confident margin may be computed from an incomplete cost total. The tile says Incomplete rather than subtracting a lower bound from revenue and printing the difference.#153 §8.5
  5. 5
    charging_summary is derived at read time for display only. It is a chip, never a field — nothing on this screen may look editable.#151 §2.4
  6. 6
    Three ceilings, each carrying basis and unit: supplier COGS, silence, duration. uncapped is a declaration a tenant makes, so it renders as a stated choice rather than a blank.#150 §2.2, §8.1 · #154
  7. 7
    Percentiles are computed over known lower bounds with every task in the sample. A p95 over “whatever happens to be in the column” is what today’s code does.#153 §11.3
  8. 8
    Margin is a bucket-level subtraction and must be able to say four different things. Three of the four appear in this one column deliberately.#153 §2, §8.5
  9. 9
    This tenant supplies revenue at customer-period grain, so it is not attributable to a kind of work. That is unavailable_at_requested_grain — a different fact from unknown, and it must not read as zero.#153 §4 · #141 §8
  10. 10
    Waived revenue is a visible exposure, not a silent zero. #147 requires it be highly visible; a footer callout is the cheapest place that satisfies that.#147 §7.3
  11. 11
    Task kinds and Subtask kinds are two segments of one registry — TaskType.kind ∈ {task, subtask}, unique on (tenant, kind, key). Separated in configuration because they are declared, ceilinged and reported independently; not split into two pages, because they are one table.#154 §3.1 · tasks/models.py:34,45-46
  12. 12
    A Subtask kind is declared, with its own COGS ceiling and its own required grouping fields. This is where a Subtask ceiling is set — there is no second configuration surface, and the resolution ladder is identical.owner ruling 2026-08-04 · risk_service.py:21,44-54
  13. 13
    Advisory dominance warning: this Subtask kind’s default ceiling (£5.00) is at or above the ceiling of a parent kind it runs inside (report-generation, £4.00), so it can never fire — the parent stops the work first. Advisory only; it never refuses.C′ · rollup is one hop (#140 §6.1, #150 §6.3)

Why each element is here

  1. 1
    A first-class object with its own route, not a settings row. It now carries a pricing mode that is snapshotted onto every task, three ceilings with their own inheritance, required grouping fields, and its own economics. A settings table cannot hold a warning, a distribution and a book cross-reference.Q1 · position taken
  2. 2
    The declaration has no publish record and no effective dating — pricing rules got one, this did not. Flipping a live kind of work silently changes the revenue shape of all future tasks, so the surface has to say so at the point of change.#151 residue, left open deliberately
  3. 3
    The kind of work declares that it is priced as a whole; it never holds the amount. The amount is a work-level line in the customer’s pricing book. Rendering it as an editable field here would be a second home for one number.#139 §3.1 · #138
  4. 4
    Q3, answered structurally: the ceiling bounds one unit of work, so it is declared on the kind of work. The other three families bound other subjects and live with them — there is no Limits page.#150 §2.2, §8.3
  5. 5
    COGS-denominated only. Customer price is not an invariant of the work, and a fixed-price counter sits at zero until delivery, so it cannot control anything while cost accumulates.#150 §3.1
  6. 6
    Ceiling shown against price for fixed kinds — the relative-tightening residue #150 handed here explicitly. This is the only place a tenant can see that their ceiling permits a loss-making delivery.#150 §17 → #152
  7. 7
    The distribution answers “what does this kind of task tend to cost us?” and nothing else. One distribution, not a resolved-only series beside a lower-bound series.#153 §11
  8. 8
    The sentence is close to verbatim from the decision, because the failure mode is a tenant reading p95 = £3.92 and setting a £4 price while the unresolved 0.7% are exactly the expensive, badly-mapped calls.#153 §11.2–11.3
  9. 9
    Composition is observed, never declared. UBB already stores every parent→child pair, so this is derived from what ran. There is no permitted-subtask-kind allowlist in v1, deliberately — an allowlist would make an agent improvising a new subtask kind into a configuration error.owner ruling 2026-08-04
  10. 10
    The dominance column is the reason this panel earns its place. It is arithmetic over two configured numbers, not a new mechanism.C′
  11. 11
    Events may be attached directly to the task without creating a subtask, and that path is a first-class row here rather than a gap. 28% of this kind’s COGS never belongs to a subtask at all.owner ruling 2026-08-04
  12. 12
    Because subtask spend rolls up one hop into the parent, a subtask ceiling at or above its parent’s can never be reached — the parent kills first and cascades downward. Advisory, because the tenant may be about to raise the parent.#140 §6.1 · #150 §2.5, §6.3
  13. 13
    Stated on the surface so nobody reads the table as a contract. It is a lagging indicator: a brand-new kind of work shows nothing here until work has run, which is the accepted cost of not declaring composition.C′ residue

Why each element is here

  1. 1
    A subtask kind is a registry row in the same table as a task kind, discriminated by TaskType.kind and unique on (tenant, kind, key). It gets its own page for the same reason a task kind does — it carries a ceiling, grouping fields and its own economics.tasks/models.py:34,45-46
  2. 2
    pricing_mode is one immutable column, parent-resolved and child-copied, so a subtask never has a say. #151 moved the priced-subtask refusal from the start gate to declaration time, which is exactly what makes this field a locked restatement rather than a control.#151 §3, §5.4 · narrows #139 §3.3
  3. 3
    The optional per-instance display label is what makes one generic kind survive contact with a run detail — without it, six agent_work rows are indistinguishable. Display-only on reason_detail’s precedent, because a free-text value that can be grouped is a cardinality explosion.owner ruling · #140 §3.3 precedent
  4. 4
    Ceilings resolve through the identical ladder as a task’s: the start call may request lower but never higher, else this kind’s default, else the tenant fallback.risk_service.py:21,44-54 · #150 §8.3
  5. 5
    The dominance warning delivered at the point of configuration rather than in a report — this is the whole of C′. It is advisory and never refuses, because the tenant may be about to raise the parent ceiling instead.C′ · owner ruling
  6. 6
    The inverse of the task kind’s composition panel, and the more useful direction when setting a ceiling: one subtask kind runs inside several parents with different ceilings, so “dominated” is per-parent, not global.C′
  7. 7
    Requiring a registered kind is a declaration-validity rule, not a composition rule — no allowlist decides which kind may run where. The generic kind is what keeps that requirement from fighting dynamic agent behaviour.owner ruling 2026-08-04
  8. 8
    Stated as a hard product rule because the inferring version is the tempting one: UBB can see provider calls and timing, and could guess boundaries. Guessed boundaries would put spend enforcement on a structure the tenant never asserted.owner ruling 2026-08-04

Why each element is here

  3. 3
    Three families named on every row, because CUSTOMER_WIDE_STOP is currently one string for two controls and “which fired?” is unanswerable.#150 §7.4, §10
  4. 4
    The pool’s basis is the charge posting — the single money path. Explaining a pool row through a usage event would imply a second route.#151 §6 · #153 §10.2

Six questions, six positions

Each of these is a proposal the wireframe commits to. The line in red is what I actually need a ruling on — the rest follows from decisions already made.

Question 1

Is a kind of work a first-class object, or a settings row?

First-class, with its own route.

It now carries a charging mode snapshotted onto every task, three ceilings with independent inheritance, required grouping fields, a cross-reference into a pricing book, and its own cost distribution. A settings table row cannot hold a change-warning, a distribution, or a ceiling-against-price gauge.

Accepted · owner review 2026-08-04. The price stays a read-only link into the book.
Question 2

What is the primary unit — kind of work, or run?

Kinds of work lands. Runs is a sibling, one click away.

A tenant opens this surface to make a decision — is this priced right, is the ceiling right, is this kind of work worth selling. Those are all aggregate questions. The run list is where you go once you already have a specific question, and it is unbounded, so it makes a poor front door.

Accepted · owner review 2026-08-04.
Question 3

Where do spend limits get set?

Wherever their subject lives. There is no Limits page.

The four families each bound a different thing: a ceiling bounds one unit of work → the kind of work. A pool bounds a customer’s spend over a period → the customer. Wallet policy → the wallet. Admission control → settings. A single limits page would re-collapse the four families #150 just separated. Reporting is centralised even though configuration is not.

Accepted · owner review 2026-08-04. Subtask ceilings resolve here too — on the declared Subtask kind.
Question 4

What does a breach look like, and what of the past-limit report?

A breach is an episode row, not a badge. The past-limit report becomes the breach report, widened to three families, and gains a sibling.

It stops being per-customer and becomes tenant-wide with a customer filter, so the customer page links into it rather than owning it. The state a task carries is killed; the episode is the record of why.

Accepted · owner review 2026-08-04. Still open: whether “past-limit report” survives as a phrase — #154’s naming question.
Question 5

How is a task broken into subtasks, and where are their ceilings set?

Declared Subtask kinds · explicitly created Subtask instances · observed composition. A two-level table with a roll-up row, never a tree widget.

A Subtask kind is a registry row with its own COGS ceiling and grouping requirements, so its ceiling is set exactly where a Task kind’s is. At runtime the orchestrator explicitly starts a Subtask against a registered kind with its own idempotency identity; events may also attach straight to the parent, and UBB never infers a boundary. Which kinds appear inside which is observed, never declared — no allowlist, so an agent improvising is data rather than an error. Unpredictable work uses a generic registered kind such as agent_work with an optional display label.

Ruled · owner 2026-08-04, intermediate model. Advisory dominance warnings only — no composition refusals. Still open: a child-count bound for the table.
Question 6

New top-level tab, or inside an existing one?

Two new top-level items: Tasks and Spend controls.

Tasks goes in the first, ungated group beside Events — every tenant has metering, the lifecycle API is top-level and ungated, and events and tasks are the two operational nouns. Spend controls goes under PLATFORM because it spans tasks, customers and wallets.

Accepted · owner review 2026-08-04.

What this prototype deliberately does not do

  • No permitted-subtask-kind allowlist. A Task kind never declares which Subtask kinds it may contain. Composition is observed from real runtime relationships, so an agent spawning a Subtask kind it has never spawned before is data, not a configuration error.
  • No inferred subtask boundaries. UBB can see provider calls and timing and could guess where a Subtask begins. It must not — a guessed boundary would put spend enforcement on a structure the tenant never asserted.
  • No composition-based start refusals. The only new refusal is declaration-validity: an explicitly created Subtask must name a registered kind. Dominance warnings are advisory and never block.
  • No dimension-scoped cap affordance. #150 §6.4 names this as a thing #152 and #153 must not build. It was never built and now never will be in v1.
  • No editable charging_summary, and no price field on the kind of work. Both are derived or owned elsewhere; making either editable creates a second home for one fact.
  • No counterfactual metered price on fixed-price work. #151 §12 declined it; the cost distribution is the replacement, and it answers a narrower question on purpose.
  • No warning threshold or amber ceiling state. Enforcement is binary and utilisation is information; v1 adds no warning event and the UI must not imply one exists.
  • No real components. Nothing here uses the console’s design system, and it should not be lifted into apps/ui — the point was the shape.

The hazard this prototype sits on

#155 recorded it plainly: #152 and #157 now prototype against a paper model. Every screen above renders a contract that exists only in decision documents — no endpoint returns a measure status, no fixture carries an indeterminate ceiling, and by #155’s count every value in the console’s 2,963 lines of mock fixtures is known. That is exactly why the wireframe is worth having before slice 0, and exactly why it cannot be trusted as a specification.
